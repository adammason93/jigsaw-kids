## 1. What you are doing

Each anchor below is one **teaching unit** from a lesson knowledge pack. A unit has two claims:
- an **element** claim, which names a feature, fact or step;
- an **explanation** claim, which should say how it works or why it leads to or matters for an outcome.

For each anchor:
1. Read the year, the teacher's request and the relation type.
2. Score **S1–S5** on the rubric in §3. Score the element and explanation **together** as one unit.
3. Give a **Pass** or **Fail** verdict using the pass rule in §3.
4. **Fact-check.** For every anchor you judge factually acceptable (S3 = 1 or 2), name the reference you checked it against, with title, URL or book, and the date you looked. If you judge an anchor inaccurate (S3 = 0), name the reference that shows this if you have one.
5. Fill in the table in §5.

There is no quota of passes or fails. Judge each anchor on its own.

All anchors are presented in a valid format: two different claims, correctly linked. **Do not score the format criteria (F1, F2).** Treat them as met.

## 2. Relation types

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `function` | A feature, and what it does for the organism or object |
| `cause_consequence` | An event or condition, and what it led to |
| `process_step` | A step in a process, and how it leads to the next step or the outcome |
| `method_reason` | A method or rule in maths or another subject, and why it works |
| `technique_effect` | A language or writing technique, and its effect on a reader |

## 3. Rubric v2.0 (frozen at calibration)

Score each criterion 0, 1 or 2. The examples are deliberately **not** taken from the anchors.

### S1: Real how or why

| Score | Meaning | Example (not an anchor) |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | **No link.** Either a purpose label ("for protection", "designed for", "adapted to") or a bare relation verb ("affects", "influences", "determines", "depends on") | "Cacti have spines for protection." / "Rainfall affects how plants grow." |
| **1** | **Names an outcome or function with no link** showing how it works or why the outcome matters. An outcome-only statement of the form "*X helped it reach / find / get Y*" scores 1 | "A cactus's spines help it survive in the desert." |
| **2** | **One concrete link** showing **how** the element works **or why** the outcome follows or matters, pitched to the year | "A cactus's sharp spines hurt animals that try to bite it, so fewer animals eat the cactus." |

**S1 calibration rules (founder):**
- An outcome-only "helped it reach / do X" statement is **S1 = 1**, however true it is.
- **S1 = 2 needs one concrete link, with no padding.** Extra clauses do **not** raise the score: adding "…and the spines also shade the stem and collect dew" to the S1 = 2 example leaves it at 2, and adding a list of outcomes to an S1 = 1 sentence leaves it at 1.
- A single link is enough at every year group. Longer chains are credited separately, not through S1.

### S2: Concrete and specific

| 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Generic on both sides ("Animals are suited to their habitats.") | A specific element **or** a specific outcome, not both ("A polar bear's fur affects how it lives.") | A specific element **and** a specific outcome that a child could picture or name ("A polar bear's thick fur traps warm air, so it stays warm on the ice.") |

### S3: Accurate and honestly scoped (fact-check required)

| 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Likely false or misleading as stated. Inverted or invented causation taught as fact. An unsourced claim tied to a specific place | Broadly right but overstated, missing a needed hedge, or loosely worded | Mainstream at Key Stage 1–2 level, hedged where experts differ, and in scope |

Your S3 score is your judgement against a named reference. It does not mark anything as "verified".

### S4: Age-fit and on-goal

| 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Off the teacher's request, or pitched more than a key stage away from the year | On the request, but uses a term the year would need explained that is not explained, or is abstract for the year | On the request and sayable by a child in that year (any technical term is explained in the claim itself) |

### S5: Distinct

| 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|
| The explanation restates the element (a synonym swap: same idea, new words) | Partial overlap: the explanation repeats much of the element and adds little | The explanation adds new how/why content beyond the element |

### Pass rule (per unit)

**Pass** only if **all** of these hold:
- **S1 = 2**;
- **no** criterion S1–S5 is 0;
- the **total S1–S5 is ≥ 8 out of 10**.

Otherwise **Fail**.

## 4. Anchors

### A1
- **Year:** Year 6
- **Teacher request:** "Year 6 geography. How do landslides happen?"
- **Relation type:** process_step
- **Element:** "Heavy rain can soak deep into a steep hillside."
- **Explanation:** "The soaked ground becomes heavier and more slippery, so it can slide downhill."

### A2
- **Year:** Year 3
- **Teacher request:** "Year 3 science. Teach children how fossils form."
- **Relation type:** process_step
- **Element:** "A fossil is the remains or trace of a living thing from long ago, preserved in rock."
- **Explanation:** "Fossils can form when a dead animal is quickly buried in mud or sand, because being buried slows down rotting."

### A3
- **Year:** Year 5
- **Teacher request:** "Year 5 history. Why did Henry VIII break with Rome?"
- **Relation type:** cause_consequence
- **Element:** "Henry VIII broke with Rome in 1534."
- **Explanation:** "Henry VIII's break with Rome was influenced by the desire for political power and independence from the Pope."

### A4
- **Year:** Year 5
- **Teacher request:** "Year 5 history. Why did Henry VIII break with Rome?"
- **Relation type:** cause_consequence
- **Element:** "One reason for the break was Henry VIII's desire for a male heir."
- **Explanation:** "Henry VIII's need for a male heir led him to seek an annulment, which the Pope denied, prompting the break."

### A5

- **Element:** "Sharks have streamlined bodies that reduce water resistance while swimming."

### A8
- **Year:** Year 4
- **Teacher request:** "Year 4 science. How are sharks adapted to living in the ocean?"
- **Relation type:** function
- **Element:** "Sharks have tiny jelly-filled pores on their snout called ampullae of Lorenzini."
- **Explanation:** "The pores sense the weak electrical signals that living animals give off, so a shark can find a fish hidden under sand."

### A9

### A10
- **Year:** Year 3
- **Teacher request:** "Year 3 science. Teach children about dinosaurs."
- **Relation type:** function
- **Element:** "Tyrannosaurus rex had about 60 large, pointed teeth, some up to 20 cm long."
- **Explanation:** "Its pointed teeth and very strong jaws gave it a bite strong enough to crunch through bone, so T. rex could eat bones as well as meat."

### A11
- **Year:** Year 2
- **Teacher request:** "Year 2 maths. Show children why adding numbers in any order gives the same answer, but subtracting does not."
- **Relation type:** method_reason
- **Element:** "Adding two numbers gives the same total in either order: 3 + 4 = 7 and 4 + 3 = 7."
- **Explanation:** "Adding puts the two groups together, and the total is the same whichever group you count first."
